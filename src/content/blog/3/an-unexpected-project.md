## Some Backstory and Context
I'm a bit of a keyboard nerd.

It all started many years ago when I got sick of hitting my mouse on that useless part of the keyboard that is a remnant of old, 80s keyboards. Yes, I'm talking about the numpad. And you either work in accounting or you got no business having a numpad nowadays. 

Oof, ok. That was a bit of a rant. Aaaany way... Back on topic.

I know it is usefull and, honestly, I do like to use it if I'm typing some bigger numbers. But for a programmer and gamer, this extra room the numpad takes in the desk becomes very annoying, very fast.

So I decided to try out some smaller keyboards. Finally decided on a TenKeyLess (TKL) keyboard. Which, as the name implies, it has ten keys less than the "classic" keyboard . The numpad, essentially. There are some other, even smaller variants, but you lose the arrows and the F numbers row. I've tried and it's as some one who programs, those are essential.

I ended up going with a Ducky One 2 TKL. It was small, relativelly cheap and very good for what I wanted. Which was gaming at the time. It came with CherryMX Speed Silver switches (relevant later on), a kind of switch that activate a lot faster - basically you need to press the key less than usual for them to activate - which is a plus in fast paced gaming where reaction time makes a difference. 

They are also very nice if you type fast. And I do. Specially while coding or even writting these blog posts.

## My Journey

Throughout the years I've tested several different keyboards and switches. Most keyboards TKL and most switches a variant of the Silver switch. 

After the Ducky keyboard I started buying Akko keyboards and switches. They are good and they are not very expensive. This led to the opportunity to review some switches

## The Oportunity that Presented Itself

In early June I got a somewhat peculiar e-mail. It came from a Keyboard manufacturer company I've been using for the past 3 or 4 years: Akko.

It was an invite to test out some new switches they were releasing in about a month. Having bought quite some switch sets from them, I know I want to try out these new switches. And for free? Absolutely!

## The Deal

Of course, there's no free meal. The free switches came with a small (I would say) catch. I need to review them! 

That's it, actually! I need to do a video review of the switches they send me. No other strings attached. No guidelines, no corporate phrase to say about them, nothing else.

I filled up a google form and a few weeks later I had two packages of 45 Akko CS Crystal Silver U1 switches in my hands.
